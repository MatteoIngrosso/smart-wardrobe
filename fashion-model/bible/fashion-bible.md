## Tips

### Colour

- Colours with some contrast to your skin and hair usually flatter; colours too close to your skin tone can wash you out. _Contrast frames the face._
- A simple colour split: about 60% a main neutral, 30% a second neutral, 10% accent. _It keeps the accent special._
- Darker trousers with a lighter top is the easiest, most flattering combination. _It grounds the outfit and lengthens the legs._
- When wearing one colour head to toe, vary the textures (knit, cotton, wool) so it doesn't look flat. _Texture adds depth where colour doesn't._
- Earth tones (olive, brown, beige, cream, rust) all mix easily with each other and with navy. _They share the same warm undertone._

### Fit

- Fit matters more than brand or price: a cheap piece that fits beats an expensive one that doesn't. _Fit is what people notice first._
- Budget for a tailor: shortening sleeves and trousers and taking in a waist costs little and makes clothes look made for you. _Off-the-rack is cut for an average body._
- A good T-shirt: sleeves end mid-bicep, shoulder seam on the shoulder, hem at the belt or just below. _Fit makes a basic tee look intentional._

### Proportions

- To look taller: one colour from shoes to waist, no-break trousers, and fitted rather than baggy pieces. _Unbroken lines lengthen the body._
- For a bigger frame: darker colours, vertical lines, structured jackets and plain fabrics rather than clingy or shiny ones. _Structure and dark colours slim the outline._

### Suits

- A jacket must fit in the shoulders: the seam ends where your shoulder ends, with no dent or overhang. _Shoulders are almost impossible to alter._
- A suit or sport jacket should cover your seat, roughly to the knuckles of your relaxed hand. _Too short looks borrowed, too long looks dated._
- Jacket sleeves end so about a centimetre of shirt cuff shows. _It frames the hands and protects the jacket cuff._
- On a two- or three-button jacket, never fasten the bottom button. On a three-button, the top one is optional. _Jackets are cut to be worn that way._
- Unbutton your jacket when you sit down and button it again when you stand. _It stops the jacket pulling and creasing._

### Shirts

- A buttoned shirt collar leaves room for one finger, no more. _Tight collars pinch, loose ones gape under a tie._
- Shirts with long curved tails get tucked in; short, straight-hemmed shirts can be worn out. _The hem tells you how the shirt was meant to be worn._
- A white or light blue oxford shirt works with suits, chinos and jeans alike. _It is the most versatile shirt a man can own._
- Under a dress shirt, wear a grey or skin-tone V-neck undershirt, not a white crew neck. _It doesn't show through or peek out at the collar._

### Knitwear

- A fine knit over a collared shirt is a smart, easy layer; keep the knit thin so the collar sits flat. _Bulky knits crush the collar._
- Knitwear should skim the body: hem at the belt, cuffs at the wrist bone. _Too long or loose looks shapeless._
- A thin turtleneck under a blazer replaces shirt and tie for an elegant evening look. _Smart without being stiff._ (Night out, Smart casual)

### Outerwear

- A coat should be longer than the jacket under it; a short jacket over a long blazer looks wrong. _Layers stack from short (inside) to long (outside)._
- A navy, camel or charcoal overcoat goes over everything from a suit to jeans and a knit. _One good coat covers most of winter._

### Trousers

- Trousers should have little or no break: the hem just touches the shoe. Slim trousers look best with no break at all. _Fabric pooling at the ankle looks sloppy._
- Chinos in navy, beige or olive bridge the gap between jeans and suit trousers. _They go with everything from a T-shirt to a blazer._

### Denim

- Dark, plain, unripped jeans are the dressiest and most versatile; light washes and rips are casual only. _Dark denim can stand in for chinos._
- Slim or straight jeans that sit on the hips without sagging work for nearly everyone. _They balance most tops and shoes._

### Shoes

- Spend on shoes and keep them clean; scuffed shoes ruin a good outfit. _Shoes are noticed more than most men think._
- From dressiest to most casual: black oxfords, brown oxfords, derbies and brogues, loafers, boots, clean leather sneakers, canvas or running shoes. _Pick the shoe from the same rung as the rest of the outfit._
- Minimal white leather sneakers can go with chinos or a casual blazer, but not with a business suit or a tie. _They are smart casual, not formal._
- Socks match the trousers (or the shoes); with shorts or loafers in summer, invisible no-show socks. _It lengthens the leg and keeps the eye moving._
- Suede shoes are less formal than polished leather and hate rain. _Texture lowers formality._
- Brown shoes with a navy or grey suit are a classic; black shoes for the most formal settings and dark suits. _Brown is warmer and more versatile; black is stricter._

### Accessories

- The tip of the tie touches the top of the belt buckle. _Shorter or longer breaks the line of the body._
- Match the width of the tie to the width of the lapels. _Matching widths keep the proportions in balance._
- A pocket square should pick up a colour from the tie or shirt, not be the same fabric as the tie. _A matching set looks bought as a kit._
- A leather watch strap looks best roughly matching belt and shoes; a metal bracelet goes with anything. _Leathers in the same family look put together._
- Keep accessories few and purposeful: a watch, a belt, maybe one bracelet or ring. _Too many pieces compete with each other._
- Pick sunglasses that contrast your face shape: angular frames for round faces, rounder frames for square faces. _Contrast balances the face._
- Take a hat off indoors; caps and beanies are casual only. _Etiquette, and caps don't work with tailoring._

### Layering

- Layer from thin to thick: tee or shirt, then knit or overshirt, then jacket, then coat. _Thin inner layers keep the outer ones from bulging._
- An overshirt is the easy layer for mild days: over a tee, open, with jeans or chinos. _Warmer than a shirt, lighter than a jacket._

### Fabrics

- Linen creases; accept it as part of the summer look rather than fighting it. _Relaxed creases are the point of linen._
- Lightweight (tropical) wool suits work in summer better than polyester ones. _Wool breathes, plastic doesn't._

### Patterns

- When combining patterns, vary their scale: a fine pattern with a bold one, never two of the same size. _Same-size patterns vibrate against each other._

### Occasions

- Business casual: chinos or wool trousers, a collared shirt or fine knit, optional blazer, leather shoes or loafers. No tie needed. _Polished but relaxed._ (Work / meeting, Smart casual)
- As a wedding guest, never outshine the groom: avoid all-white, keep it a suit unless the invitation says otherwise. _The day isn't about you._ (Elegant)
- For a date or a night out, go one notch smarter than the venue: a good knit or shirt, dark trousers, clean leather shoes. _It shows effort without trying too hard._ (Night out)

### Formal

- Black tie means a black or midnight-navy dinner suit, white dress shirt, black bow tie and black patent or polished shoes. _It is a fixed dress code, not a suggestion._ (Elegant)
- Business formal: a dark suit in navy or charcoal, white or light blue shirt, a quiet tie, dark leather shoes. _Conservative colours read as serious._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)

### Smart casual

- Smart casual: one tailored element (a blazer, a crisp shirt or wool trousers) mixed with relaxed pieces like dark jeans or clean sneakers. _The mix is what makes it smart casual._ (Smart casual, Night out)

### Casual

- A polo dresses up a T-shirt outfit instantly; wear it with chinos or shorts, tucked or untucked if short. _The collar adds polish._

### Streetwear

- In streetwear, build around one hero piece (sneakers, a graphic hoodie, a jacket) and keep the rest plain. _The hero piece needs space._
- Oversized streetwear tops work with straight or tapered trousers; if both are baggy, keep the colours simple. _Volume plus busy colours turns messy._
- Big logos look young and loud; quieter pieces with small or no logos look more grown-up. _Let the cut and colour speak._

### Sportswear

- Joggers and hoodies outside the gym look best when they are clean, well fitting and in plain colours. _Sloppy sportswear looks like you just woke up._
- Train in proper training shoes and save fashion sneakers for the street. _Support for the feet, and the sneakers last longer._ (Gym)

### Wardrobe

- A capsule wardrobe: a few pieces in neutral colours that all combine gives more outfits than many loud pieces. _Every piece works with every other._
- Core menswear basics: white and navy tees, an oxford shirt, a grey or navy knit, dark jeans, chinos, a navy blazer, an overcoat, white sneakers, brown leather shoes or boots. _These combine into dozens of outfits._
- Buy fewer, better pieces in natural fabrics; they last longer and look better as they age. _Cost per wear matters more than price._

### Care

- Fold knitwear, never hang it; wash wool cold and dry it flat. _Hanging stretches the shoulders out of shape._
- Rest leather shoes a day between wears and keep cedar shoe trees in them. _They dry out and keep their shape._
- Wrinkled shirts and trousers look careless whatever their price; steam or iron before wearing. _Crisp fabric reads as effort._
