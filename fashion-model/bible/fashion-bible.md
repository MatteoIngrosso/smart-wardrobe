## Tips

### Colour

- When wearing one colour head to toe, vary the textures (knit, cotton, wool) so it doesn't look flat. _Texture adds depth where colour doesn't._ (3 sources)
- Earth tones (olive, brown, beige, cream, rust) all mix easily with each other and with navy. _They share the same warm undertone._ (3 sources)
- Colours with some contrast to your skin and hair usually flatter; colours too close to your skin tone can wash you out. _Contrast frames the face._ (2 sources)
- A simple colour split: about 60% a main neutral, 30% a second neutral, 10% accent. _It keeps the accent special._
- Darker trousers with a lighter top is the easiest, most flattering combination. _It grounds the outfit and lengthens the legs._
- Black and white together is a timeless palette that suits classic, tailored or minimalist dressing. _The stark contrast reads as clean and high quality._ (Smart casual, Night out, Elegant)
- Build colour pairings on contrast between a light shade and a dark one. _Light-dark contrast is what most reliable combinations have in common._
- Keep the tie darker than the shirt; avoid a light tie on a dark shirt. _A pale tie on a dark shirt looks cheap._ (Work / meeting, Smart casual, Night out)
- Wearing one rich dark colour such as brown head to toe can feel heavy; add a white or light piece to lift it. _A light element breaks up a dense block of colour._
- A matching sweatsuit only works when top and bottom are truly the same shade; near-misses look accidental. _Slightly different tones of one colour clash._ (Everyday)
- Think about colour before buying a layer: a brown zip-up goes with darker or bootcut jeans and a white tee more easily than with a busy graphic. _Check new pieces against what you will wear them with._ (Everyday)
- Use contrast between shirt and trousers: a dark shirt with light trousers or a white shirt with dark trousers. _Contrast gives an outfit definition._
- If you work behind the scenes or want to blend in, an all-black outfit of simple basics is practical and always looks put together. _Black basics are discreet and easy to combine._ (Everyday)
- Minimalism is about removing visual noise, not only wearing black: grey, brown, navy, cream, olive or burgundy work as long as pieces don't compete. _Silhouette, fabric and the wearer matter more than decoration._
- Wear black as the main colour with camel as the accent, roughly a 70/30 split, for example an all-black outfit under a camel coat. _Striking contrast with very little risk._ (Everyday, Smart casual)
- If brown feels hard to wear, choose camel instead of dark brown; it is lighter and sits easily with black, white and grey. _Lighter browns are more forgiving than dark chocolate shades._
- Pick camel pieces in wool or other textured fabrics. _Texture adds depth to a plain, light colour._
- Pair deep navy with burgundy rather than bright blue with purple. _The dark versions look rich and smart; the bright ones look harsh, like a bruise._ (Smart casual, Night out)
- Wear a burgundy roll neck under a navy suit, or tucked into pleated trousers, for a smart pairing without a shirt and tie. _Navy grounds the look and burgundy adds richness._ (Smart casual, Night out)
- Ground dark green trousers with brown shoes such as brogues or derbies. _Green and brown are a natural pairing and dark green suits most builds._ (Smart casual, Everyday)
- Combine pink with soft neutrals, especially grey, rather than with other strong colours. _The neutral calms the pink so it doesn't overwhelm the outfit._ (Everyday, Smart casual)
- Avoid wearing red and green together. _The pairing looks like a Christmas costume._
- In a black and white outfit, aim for about 70/30: one piece in one colour and the rest in the other, rather than alternating pieces head to toe. _Alternating black and white items looks like zebra stripes._
- Try navy with cream for smart casual outfits, such as an unstructured navy blazer with cream chinos. _It has the contrast of black and white but feels softer._ (Smart casual, Work / meeting)
- For autumn and winter, wear brown with beige as a tonal pairing, such as brown outerwear over beige trousers. _The two shades sit together naturally in the colder months._ (Everyday)
- Break up a brown and beige outfit with white, or a touch of muted green or burnt orange. _A small contrast stops a tonal look from going flat._ (Everyday)
- Pair dark green with black, especially in tailoring, for example a green blazer over a black roll neck for cocktail attire. _Two dark colours together look rich and evening-appropriate._ (Night out, Elegant)
- Use olive with white in summer, dressed up with tailoring or down with a tee and cargo trousers. _The pairing is fresh and works at both ends of the formality scale._ (Everyday, Elegant)
- When combining brown and green, add white accents such as white sneakers. _Without some white the earthy pairing can look dull and dated._ (Everyday)
- For an easy low-risk outfit, mix different shades of grey, starting with dark charcoal against light grey. _Greys always sit together; the shade difference keeps it interesting._ (Everyday, Smart casual, Work / meeting)
- Wear pink with blue in pale or pastel shades for casual spring and summer looks, not in the most saturated tones. _Bright versions of the two colours tend to clash._ (Everyday)
- Put a bold colour in a base or middle layer, soften it with neutral outer layers, and echo it in socks or sneaker details. _The colour shows without overwhelming the outfit._ (Everyday)
- With brightly coloured chinos, keep the rest of the outfit neutral. _One strong colour is enough._ (Everyday)
- Use festive reds and greens in muted, deep shades rather than bright ones. _Bright seasonal colours quickly look clownish._ (Elegant, Night out)
- Wear blue tonally, but don't pair a jacket and trousers in two different blues for formal wear. _Two near-matching blues look like a mistake rather than a choice._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Treat neutrals as more than beige and camel; wear brighter, lighter neutrals in summer. _They reflect light and feel fresher in the heat._ (Everyday, Smart casual)
- Add colour with contrasting shades while keeping the overall look controlled. _Contrast works when the rest stays balanced._
- Repeat your sneakers' colours in the shirt and tie. _Echoed colours tie the outfit together and make the pairing look intentional._ (Smart casual)
- If you wear navy trousers with a jacket, choose a muted, greyish jacket such as charcoal tweed, never a rich or bright one. _Navy is a rich colour and clashes with other strong colours._ (Smart casual)
- Match jeans to the jacket: dark blue denim with brown, beige, green or blue blazers; black denim with grey or charcoal ones. _These pairings keep the colours harmonious._ (Smart casual, Everyday)
- If the blazer is smooth and plain, keep the outfit tonal, such as a navy blazer with a navy knit and dark denim. _Tonal colour stops a flat jacket looking like it lost its trousers._ (Smart casual)
- Work out your palette from your undertone, how light or deep your colouring is, and how muted or clear it is; a professional draping session helps. _It removes the guesswork from choosing colours._ (Everyday, Smart casual, Work / meeting)
- Get colour right first in pieces worn close to the face: shirts, knitwear, glasses frames and hats. _They decide whether you look healthy or tired._ (Everyday, Smart casual, Work / meeting)
- Trousers and shoes can stray further from your best colours than tops can. _They sit low, away from the face, so they affect your complexion less._ (Everyday, Smart casual, Work / meeting)
- Match your outfit's contrast to your own: strong dark-and-light colouring suits dramatic dark tailoring, softer colouring suits muted slate and charcoal. _Clothes that are far bolder or softer than your features overwhelm or flatten you._ (Work / meeting, Smart casual)
- Notice which colours bring you compliments; they usually point to your best palette. _Other people judge your face more objectively than you do._ (Everyday, Smart casual)
- In daytime heat wear breathable fabrics in lighter tones; save richer, darker colours for the evening. _Light colours stay cooler, and dark ones feel right after dark._ (Everyday, Night out)
- Colours in the same family as your hair and skin tone, such as browns and olives on warm colouring, can look especially rich. _Tonal harmony with your colouring looks deliberate._ (opinion)
- Light colours are fine in winter; a military-style coat keeps a pale outfit looking tough. _Season does not have to mean dark colours only._ (opinion; Everyday)
- Don't lean on the old complementary colour-wheel rule; use proven pairings such as black and camel, navy and burgundy, green and brown, pink and grey. _Opposite colours often clash, and tested pairings are safer._ (opinion)
- With a green suit, try a brown knitted tie. _It repeats the green-brown pairing in a relaxed texture._ (opinion; Smart casual, Elegant)
- Avoid bright suits, loud ties and socks, and tan shoes at a wedding. _Elegance comes from cut, texture and restrained colour, not loudness._ (opinion; Elegant)
- When building jackets and trousers to mix, keep jackets darker and trousers lighter. _That pattern makes jackets, trousers and shoes easier to combine._ (opinion; Smart casual, Work / meeting)

### Fit

- Budget for a tailor: shortening sleeves and trousers and taking in a waist costs little and makes clothes look made for you. _Off-the-rack is cut for an average body._ (5 sources)
- Fit matters more than brand or price: a cheap piece that fits beats an expensive one that doesn't. _Fit is what people notice first._ (4 sources)
- A jacket collar should rest against the back of your neck without a gap. _A gaping collar is a basic sign of a poorly fitting jacket._ (4 sources)
- A good T-shirt: sleeves end mid-bicep, shoulder seam on the shoulder, hem at the belt or just below. _Fit makes a basic tee look intentional._ (2 sources)
- Buy jackets and shirts that fit the shoulders and chest, and trousers that fit the seat and thighs; most other things can be altered. _Those areas are hard or impossible for a tailor to fix._ (2 sources)
- Don't make everything skin tight: let jeans fall straight from the hip, chinos have room in the thigh and an oxford blouse slightly at the waist. _A little ease looks more natural and current than vacuum-sealed slim fits._ (2 sources; Everyday, Smart casual)
- If you size up a T-shirt, have the sleeves shortened. _Wide, long sleeves flare out like an umbrella._ (2 sources; Everyday)
- Don't size down to look slimmer. _Tight clothes tend to make you look heavier, not thinner._ (2 sources)
- Check that fabric hangs cleanly: pulling lines across chest or waist, or bunched folds, mean the fit is wrong. _Tension and excess cloth are the clearest signs of poor fit._
- Check the jacket's back: apart from slight folds under the arms it should be smooth, free of horizontal or vertical wrinkles. _A clean back is the best overall sign that the jacket fits._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Buttoned, the front should form a clean V above and fall slightly open below; X-shaped pulls mean too tight, vertical folds too loose. _These creases reveal fit at a glance._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- When between sizes, take the larger one. _Taking a garment in is much easier than letting it out._
- Wear the shoes and layers you'll pair with a garment to fittings: dress shoes and shirt for a suit, a sweater under a coat. _Lengths and ease are set against what you'll actually wear._
- Lapels should lie flat against the chest without gaping or lifting. _Gaping lapels make even a costly suit look cheap._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- The jacket should button without pulling, leaving room to slide a flat hand inside. _Too tight causes strain lines; too loose looks sloppy._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Read the jacket's back: horizontal creases mean too tight, vertical folds mean excess cloth, and spreading vents mean tight hips or seat. _The back exposes fit problems that are hard to hide._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Choose the size that fits shoulders, chest and seat, then have the waist, sleeves and trouser length tailored. _Structural changes are costly and rarely succeed, and making a suit smaller is easier than larger._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Choose clothes for your own build rather than copying a celebrity's exact cut; slimmer, broader or shorter men need different proportions. _The goal is the effect, not someone else's measurements._
- Know your measurements and what fits your body; a lean frame of average height looks best between tailored and relaxed, not skin tight. _Clothes with shape and some ease look natural rather than tense._
- Tight all-black clothes such as skinny black jeans, a tight shirt and a cropped jacket miss the point of minimalism; the clothes need air around the body. _Colour alone doesn't make the look; silhouette does._
- Check a crew-neck sweatshirt's fit: neckline at the start of the shoulders, ribbing neither tight nor long, and shape recovering after you raise your arms. _Poorly made sweatshirts lose their shape fast._ (Everyday)
- Shorts should end around the knee, without bulky extra pockets. _That length and a clean leg look neater._ (Everyday)
- Aim for a moderate fit, neither very slim nor very baggy. _Both extremes are common mistakes; a middle fit is a safe baseline._
- Test fit while moving: a shirt shouldn't strain when you sit, and a jacket should let you lift your arms. _Clothes have to work in motion, not just standing in a mirror._
- Train your eye by studying outfits you admire: where seams fall, how cloth drapes and where garments end. _Recognising good fit and proportion takes years of looking._
- Wear new interview clothes a few times beforehand and leave two to three weeks for alterations. _Comfortable, well-fitting clothes help you feel confident._ (Work / meeting)
- Choose trouser width for your frame: wide legs can swamp a very slim man, while tight tapers make sitting uncomfortable. _Proportion and practicality matter more than the trend._
- Prefer jackets with higher, closer armholes; oversized armholes make the whole jacket lift and shift when you raise your arms. _A snug armhole actually gives more freedom of movement and a cleaner line._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Leave a little clean drape over the chest; a chest cut too tight makes the lapels roll and pleats form. _Some ease looks natural and lets the lapels lie flat._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Sleeves should hang straight without twisting wrinkles; if they twist, have a tailor correct the sleeve pitch. _Pitch errors are fixable and otherwise spoil the line of the arm._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- A buttoned jacket should still give about 5 cm when pulled away from the stomach. _That ease lets you move without the jacket looking loose._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Try a new tailor on a simple job, such as hemming chinos, before trusting them with a jacket. _A cheap, low-risk job shows whether their work is good._
- A dent just below the shoulder pad usually means the shoulders are too narrow; size up rather than paying to fix it. _The fix is expensive and can create new problems._ (Work / meeting, Smart casual)
- Don't have everything taken in too slim, the most common mistake of new tailoring customers. _Over-tight clothes restrict movement and look strained._
- Make irreversible alterations in small steps and live with each change before going further. _Cut fabric can't be put back._
- Allow for movement: shirt cuffs should stay at the wrist when you reach, and trousers shouldn't be hemmed too short as they ride up. _Clothes are judged moving, not only standing still._
- Judge a suit while standing relaxed, not while holding your breath or striking a pose. _Only your natural posture shows how it really fits._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Treat dimples at the top of the sleeve as a warning sign of a poor shoulder fit or a cut that doesn't suit your posture. _They rarely disappear and spoil the shoulder line._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Test a suit by reaching, walking and sitting; it should settle back into place without constant tugging. _A suit that fights your movements doesn't fit._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Check the jacket unbuttoned as well; it should still look sharp worn open. _Most men wear their jacket open much of the time._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Avoid jackets cut too short or too tight and trousers whose waist sits too low. _These common mistakes make a suit look juvenile or sloppy._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Choose a hoodie with a neat, fitted cut instead of a loose, sagging one. _A sharper fit looks intentional, not sloppy._ (Everyday)
- Give skinny fits shape with sharp elements: dress shoes, tops with defined shoulders and waist, or a slight flare at the hem. _They stop a slim outfit reading as one straight, thin line._ (Everyday, Smart casual)
- Don't overdo looseness; an outfit that is baggy everywhere can look as if you didn't try. _Too much volume reads sloppy rather than relaxed._ (Everyday)
- Anchor a baggy outfit at the waist: tuck the top in and add a belt. _A defined middle makes the look deliberate and shows your shape._ (Everyday, Smart casual)
- A boxy T-shirt should fall at the waist with sleeves reaching below mid-upper-arm; very short sleeves look shrunken on a boxy cut. _Boxy proportions need length in the sleeve._ (opinion; Everyday)
- Wide-leg trousers flatter a broader range of builds than slim fits. _Their extra room is more forgiving._ (opinion)
- Choose how tight or loose to wear clothes by your body and taste, not by what the current trend dictates. _Fit that suits you outlasts any trend cycle._ (opinion; Everyday, Smart casual)

### Proportions

- To look taller: one colour from shoes to waist, no-break trousers, and fitted rather than baggy pieces. _Unbroken lines lengthen the body._ (2 sources)
- Match the looseness of top and bottom: don't pair loose trousers with a tight shirt or the other way round. _Consistent volume keeps the silhouette coherent._ (2 sources)
- For a bigger frame: darker colours, vertical lines, structured jackets and plain fabrics rather than clingy or shiny ones. _Structure and dark colours slim the outline._
- Choose controlled volume: trousers with room through the hip that taper gently, and relaxed jackets that still follow the body. _It sits between 2010s skinny and today's oversized extremes._
- If you are slim, avoid oversized collars and very wide lapels. _Big details overwhelm a slight frame._
- If you are stocky, avoid skinny ties and narrow collars. _Small details make a broad frame look bigger by contrast._
- Wear trousers sitting a couple of inches below the navel and tuck in your shirt or knit. _A higher waistline makes the legs look longer._
- Short men should choose slim or tapered cuts over baggy or boxy ones. _Excess fabric swamps a small frame and makes it look smaller._
- Keep trousers plain and neutral and put pattern and detail on your top half. _Detail near the face draws the eye upward; loud trousers pull it down._
- Short men suit a slim suit with narrow lapels and the jacket button placed slightly above the navel. _One column of colour and a high button point lengthen the body._ (Work / meeting, Smart casual, Elegant)
- Choose shirts that end around the hips rather than long, boxy ones. _A shorter hem shows more leg; boxy cuts look dumpy._
- Favour cropped jackets such as a Harrington or bomber, and keep overcoats above mid-thigh. _Long coats swamp a short frame like a cape._
- Judge jacket length by balance: back collar to hem should roughly equal hem to trouser bottom, which normally still covers the seat. _Balanced proportions outlast trends; jackets too short or too long look off._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Place the jacket button at the natural waist; shorter men may raise it slightly, but the tie should never show below it. _The buttoning point sets your proportions, and a tie tip peeking out looks sloppy._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Balance wide trousers with a roomy, oversized top. _A slim top over wide legs makes the torso look small; a loose top keeps the proportions even._ (Everyday)
- When mixing tight and loose, let one half lead: an oversized tee over slim jeans, or a cropped boxy hoodie over barrel-leg trousers. _Contrasting volumes look intentional when one piece clearly dominates._ (Everyday)
- For extra height, pick chunky-soled trainers, commando-soled shoes or work boots rather than Cuban heels. _They add height quietly; obvious heels can look desperate._ (opinion)
- With wide or barrel-leg trousers, either balance with a neater, smaller top or commit to an equally slouchy one. _Every layer's shape changes the overall silhouette._ (opinion; Everyday, Smart casual)
- Wide-leg trousers and relaxed blazers work especially well with sneakers. _Loose tailoring shares the ease of casual shoes._ (opinion; Smart casual, Everyday)
- A double-breasted navy blazer adds structure and can make you look taller and broader; it suits most builds. _Its overlapping front and strong lines sharpen the silhouette._ (opinion; Smart casual, Work / meeting)
- Play a hoodie against wide-leg trousers or a long formal coat for high-low contrast. _The mix of casual and formal looks deliberate._ (opinion; Everyday, Smart casual)

### Suits

- A jacket must fit in the shoulders: the seam ends where your shoulder ends, with no dent or overhang. _Shoulders are almost impossible to alter._ (3 sources)
- A suit or sport jacket should cover your seat, roughly to the knuckles of your relaxed hand. _Too short looks borrowed, too long looks dated._ (2 sources)
- Jacket sleeves end so about a centimetre of shirt cuff shows. _It frames the hands and protects the jacket cuff._ (2 sources)
- On a two- or three-button jacket, never fasten the bottom button. On a three-button, the top one is optional. _Jackets are cut to be worn that way._ (2 sources)
- Have a suit jacket gently taken in at the waist and keep it buttoned when standing, so it shows a waist instead of hanging like a box. _A defined waist flatters every build._ (2 sources; Work / meeting, Elegant)
- A plain office suit such as a pinstripe stops looking dull when paired with a more interesting shirt and tie combination. _Shirt and tie carry the personality of a conservative suit._ (2 sources; Work / meeting)
- Unbutton your jacket when you sit down and button it again when you stand. _It stops the jacket pulling and creasing._
- A black suit is traditionally evening wear; for daytime most men look better in navy or grey. _Black is harsh in daylight and unflattering on many._ (Work / meeting)
- Start with silhouette: high-waisted trousers with a crease and a jacket with a defined shoulder give tailoring a strong frame. _Proportion is the foundation of good tailoring._ (Work / meeting, Night out)
- For hot weather, choose half-canvas, light jackets with a light silk or open shirt so a suit can go from lunch to dinner. _Light construction keeps tailoring wearable in heat._ (Smart casual, Night out)
- Choose suit cloth with enough weight to drape; in summer, wear lighter linen and cotton suits in pale colours. _The right fabric keeps a suit from looking flat or cheap._ (Elegant, Work / meeting)
- For a summer suit that wrinkles less than pure linen, choose a linen-silk blend; the jacket and trousers also work as separates. _Silk reduces creasing and the pieces can be reworn with jeans or a tee._ (Elegant, Smart casual)
- Wool-mohair is a light, crisp suiting with a slight sheen, a good alternative to tropical wool for warm-weather events. _Mohair adds lightness and resists creasing._ (Elegant, Night out)
- Half-lined or unlined jackets are cooler and move more easily; unlined construction needs cleaner internal seams. _Less lining means more comfort in heat._
- Prefer canvassed jackets over fused ones; a full canvas keeps its shape for years, while fused jackets lose structure over time. _Sewn canvas lets the cloth drape and last._
- Flap pockets suit business suits, jetted pockets are more ceremonial, and patch pockets are the casual choice for blazers. _Pocket style sets a jacket's formality._ (Work / meeting, Elegant, Smart casual)
- Choose shoulder construction to suit your posture and build: padded for structure, soft or Neapolitan for a relaxed look. _The shoulder defines the silhouette._
- A suit should feel relaxed rather than tight, with no pulling across the back or at the armholes. _A suit that strains looks borrowed._
- Dress down a flannel suit by swapping shirt and tie for a cashmere roll-neck and suede shoes. _It stays elegant while feeling relaxed and smart casual._ (Smart casual)
- In a business-casual workplace, save the full suit for board meetings and job interviews. _A suit every day looks overdressed in a relaxed office._ (Work / meeting)
- Navy and charcoal suits are the most versatile wedding-guest choices, in lighter or heavier wool depending on the season. _Sober dark colours fit almost any dress code._ (Elegant)
- For a cocktail-attire wedding, wear a dark grey or navy suit with a white or light blue shirt, or dark brown for an outdoor daytime event. _Dark suits meet the code without straying into black tie._ (Elegant)
- Choose a wedding suit that looks less like business wear, such as light grey or double-breasted. _It marks the day as a special occasion._ (Elegant)
- A navy suit looks less like office wear when it is double-breasted or in a textured cloth such as herringbone. _Cut and texture take it away from the business uniform._ (Elegant)
- Have your key suits and jackets made to measure, choosing the cloth and details yourself. _Tailoring cut to your body makes a visible difference._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Dress a suit down with soft, unlined construction and espadrilles or yacht loafers instead of formal shoes. _It takes the stiffness out for lunches or dates._ (Smart casual, Night out)
- Choose side vents ending around pocket level; centre vents add little, and ventless jackets suit evening wear only if not tight. _Side vents flatter most figures and let the jacket hang cleanly in movement._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Wear a two-piece jacket buttoned when standing, but a three-piece jacket may be left open over the waistcoat. _The waistcoat already shapes the torso._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Don't pay to restyle details such as shoulder structure or lapel width; buy the style you want instead. _It is risky, costly and rarely looks right, though removing a jacket's back lining is usually fine._ (Work / meeting, Smart casual)
- Consider charcoal rather than black for a suit. _Charcoal sits more softly against the face and pairs with more colours._ (Work / meeting, Elegant)
- A plain T-shirt under a suit can look great if you wear it with ease. _Confidence and comfort sell the look._ (2 sources; opinion; Night out, Smart casual)
- Wide lapels and a structured, padded shoulder give a suit a strong, sharp silhouette, especially on a tall slim frame. _Structure adds presence._ (opinion; Work / meeting, Elegant)
- Pinstripe spacing changes the mood: wide and bold looks like a banker, very close looks slick. _Pattern scale sets formality._ (opinion; Work / meeting)
- Wear a suit like ordinary clothes and stay relaxed in it; don't let it make you stiff. _You should wear the suit, not the other way round._ (opinion)
- A well-made custom suit does more for your wardrobe than a rare, expensive pair of sneakers. _Tailoring is useful far more often than a collector's shoe._ (opinion)
- Double-breasted jackets look right with wide peak lapels and enough structure, ideally full canvas. _A DB needs structure to hold its shape._ (opinion; Elegant, Work / meeting)
- Statement suits in velvet, metallic or beaded fabrics are red-carpet pieces; they work only if you have the confidence and the occasion for them. _Bold cloth demands the right setting._ (opinion; Elegant, Night out)
- For the smartest suits, choose trousers without belt loops, using side adjusters or braces. _A belt-free waist gives a cleaner line._ (opinion; Work / meeting, Elegant)
- To show off good tailoring, choose a plain mid-toned flannel suit. _The rich, plain cloth draws the eye to the lapels, shoulders and chest._ (opinion; Work / meeting, Elegant)
- At a conservative office holiday party, pair a dark suit with a pastel shirt rather than plain white. _A white shirt can look stuffy at a celebration._ (opinion; Work / meeting, Elegant)

### Shirts

### Outerwear

- Line an overcoat in silk or a slippery synthetic rather than cashmere; get warmth from a heavier outer cloth instead. _A smooth lining slides over jacket sleeves, while a fuzzy one catches._
- Make sure the overcoat collar stands high enough at the back to cover your shirt collar. _Otherwise rain runs onto the shirt collar._
- A second-hand coat a couple of inches too big in the chest can usually be taken in, and a belt hides leftover looseness. _Good vintage coats are worth adjusting rather than passing over._
- Skip technical mountain or sports jackets at the office; a trench or mac covers cool, wet days. _Performance outerwear looks out of place over office clothes._ (Work / meeting)
- Dress up a rugged suede jacket with a crisp white shirt. _The clean shirt makes it fit for smarter settings._ (Smart casual)
- For a first overshirt, a military-style cotton one in olive or khaki is a safe pick. _It reflects the garment's fatigue-shirt origins and goes with most casual clothes._ (Everyday)
- Save raglan sleeves for raincoats; on a smart overcoat they lose too much shape. _Without a defined shoulder line the coat looks shapeless and less elegant._ (opinion)
- A sharply roped, structured shoulder suits a coat worn with tailoring, not one worn with denim and boots. _Its formality clashes with rugged casual clothes._ (opinion)
- For a first overcoat, navy is the most versatile colour, pale grey next, camel the least. _Navy sits comfortably over the widest range of outfits._ (opinion)
- For streetwear outerwear, a long parka covers winter, an aviator bomber the cold, and a varsity jacket a street look. _Each suits the style and the season._ (opinion; Everyday)
- For daytime, pair blue trousers with a beige or off-white soft-collared safari jacket in cotton or cotton-linen. _Light jacket over darker trousers looks relaxed and summery._ (opinion; Smart casual, Everyday)
- Try a soft suede jacket over sharp tailoring. _The relaxed texture contrasts strikingly with a crisp suit._ (opinion; Smart casual)
- Break up a heavy suede coat with patterned trousers. _Pattern gives a classic suede piece a modern edge._ (opinion; Everyday)
- Leave the cuffs of a suede jacket open or roll the sleeves. _It gives an easy, slightly careless feel that suits the material._ (opinion; Everyday)
- A suede trench in a versatile shade such as dusty tan makes a long-term outerwear investment. _A neutral suede coat gets better with age and goes with much._ (opinion)

### Formal

- Black tie means a black or midnight-navy dinner suit, white dress shirt, black bow tie and black patent or polished shoes. _It is a fixed dress code, not a suggestion._ (3 sources; Elegant)

- For smart casual, pick a soft, unstructured blazer over a stiff, formal one. _A rigid blazer looks like office tailoring and fights the relaxed pieces._ (Smart casual)

- To ease away from suits, start with chinos and a shirt with the sleeves rolled up. _It is a gentle first step toward relaxed dressing._ (Everyday, Smart casual)

### Wardrobe

- Buy fewer, better pieces in natural fabrics; they last longer and look better as they age. _Cost per wear matters more than price._ (6 sources)
- A capsule wardrobe: a few pieces in neutral colours that all combine gives more outfits than many loud pieces. _Every piece works with every other._ (4 sources)
- Core menswear basics: white and navy tees, an oxford shirt, a grey or navy knit, dark jeans, chinos, a navy blazer, an overcoat, white sneakers, brown leather shoes or boots. _These combine into dozens of outfits._ (4 sources)
- Build a wardrobe slowly, one considered piece at a time, rather than in one big online order. _You learn what works and buy less._ (2 sources)
- Breaking rules works only when you know them; whatever you wear, put deliberate thought into it. _Intent is what makes an outfit look stylish._ (2 sources)
- Spend selectively: more on a jacket, coat or boots you'll wear for years, less on basic T-shirts. _Quality matters most where wear is heaviest._ (2 sources)
- A 'Made in Italy' label often only means the garment was sewn there; it doesn't guarantee Italian fabric or better quality than good makers elsewhere, such as in Portugal. _Judge the garment, not just the country on the tag._ (2 sources)
- Don't make classic clothes look too perfect or bought together; a slightly careless, accumulated look reads more stylish. _Clothes that look lived in seem effortless._ (3 sources; opinion)
- Dress for the job and life you are aiming for, not the one you had at school; clothes signal whether people can trust you with responsibility. _People judge competence partly by appearance._ (Work / meeting)
- Choose pieces that get better with age: a suede jacket, a slightly rumpled linen shirt, an olive field jacket, brown loafers that have been walked in. _Clothes that acquire character look personal._ (Everyday)
- When buying vintage or second-hand, check the garment's measurements rather than the size on the label, and favour natural fabrics. _Sizing changes over the years; natural fibres last._
- Shop for specific ingredients, such as a brown herringbone sport coat or a navy lambswool crew neck, rather than a brand or logo; second-hand is fine. _The look comes from the pieces, not the label._
- When building a wardrobe from scratch, balance categories: a few bottoms, a few tops, a layer and one pair of shoes, rather than many of one thing. _A balanced set makes more outfits._ (Everyday)
- Skip colours you already own several of; add pieces that expand your rotation instead. _Duplicates add little new outfit potential._
- Aim to look exceptionally well dressed while seeming barely to have tried: precise, understated clothes with no unnecessary details. _Restraint reads as confidence._
- Don't wear every piece associated with a style icon at once; the result looks like cosplay. _Subtle inspiration looks stylish; a full copy looks like a costume._
- Clear out clothes that are worn out, dated or never suited you, and sell or donate them. _A smaller wardrobe of things you'll wear is easier to dress from._
- Shop for the life you actually lead and what you wear most, not trends or a lifestyle you don't have. _Clothes that suit your habits get worn; the rest sits unused._
- While you're still learning, buy mid-priced pieces and treat early purchases as experiments. _Your taste will change and you'll likely replace them._
- Look at second-hand and thrift shops, locally or online, for well-made clothes. _Good pieces can be found for much less._
- Never buy something just because it's on sale. _A discount doesn't make an unneeded or ill-fitting piece useful._
- Put money into timeless staples such as a wool coat and good leather shoes rather than passing trends. _They last longer and never go out of date._
- Plan your outfits ahead for a busy summer social calendar. _Packed weeks of events are easy when the right clothes are ready._ (Elegant)
- Move toward casual clothes step by step from your comfort zone, not in one sudden jump. _An abrupt switch, like monk straps straight to sneakers, looks borrowed._ (Everyday)
- Buy a hoodie in a timeless design and a colour that suits your wardrobe rather than a trend. _You'll get far more wear from it._ (Everyday)
- Once you know your colours, buy fewer pieces in them and keep them for years. _Clothes in flattering colours get worn, which saves time and money._ (Everyday, Smart casual, Work / meeting)
- A personal uniform works for a public figure, but most men are better served by a variety of outfits for different occasions and moods. _Real life needs different clothes for different situations._ (opinion)
- Don't panic when an outfit feels simple; three very good pieces can be enough. _Adding more stops adding to the person._ (opinion)
- Don't assume a brand collaboration is special; judge the garment itself. _Many collaborations are mainly marketing._ (opinion)
- Think about what an outfit says as a whole; changing one piece changes its message. _Dressing works like writing: every element contributes._ (opinion)

### Care

- Rest leather shoes a day between wears and keep cedar shoe trees in them. _They dry out and keep their shape._ (4 sources)
- Fold knitwear, never hang it; wash wool cold and dry it flat. _Hanging stretches the shoulders out of shape._ (2 sources)
- Wrinkled shirts and trousers look careless whatever their price; steam or iron before wearing. _Crisp fabric reads as effort._ (2 sources)
- Read care labels first: most clothes can be washed at home, but tailored jackets and suits usually need dry cleaning. _Washing tailoring can ruin its structure._
- Sort laundry into whites, colours and delicates, and wash each load separately. _Mixing them leads to dye transfer and damaged delicate fabrics._
- Wash new dark or indigo pieces on their own for the first few washes. _Fresh dyes bleed onto other clothes._
- Avoid the tumble dryer. _Its heat shrinks and wears out clothes._
- Hang tailored jackets and overcoats on broad, correctly sized hangers, never wire hangers or coat pegs. _Poor support distorts the shoulders._
- Clean tailoring before storing it for long, and keep it in breathable, moth-proof garment bags. _Moths go for dirty wool, and plastic traps moisture._
- Match fragrance to the season: light, fresh scents such as citrus for hot days, stronger sweet scents for cold weather and evenings. _Heavy scents overwhelm in heat; light ones vanish in the cold._
- If you own only one fragrance, make it a fresh, not-too-loud one that suits every occasion. _Smelling fresh is never wrong; smelling very sweet often is._
- Add a sweeter, warmer fragrance as your second, for evenings, dates and winter. _Two scents cover day and night._ (Night out)
- Don't wear a heavy sweet fragrance to the gym or in close quiet places like a library. _Strong scent in the wrong place bothers people around you._ (Gym)
- Spray strong scents about three times (sides of the neck and a wrist), lighter fresh scents about five, and adjust to each fragrance. _Concentration varies, so dose accordingly._
- Use an unscented deodorant so it doesn't clash with your fragrance. _Competing scents muddy each other._
- EDT, EDP and extrait are concentrations; higher usually lasts longer, but versions with the same name often smell different, so test each. _The label doesn't guarantee the same scent._
- Dry-clean a suit rarely, about once a season, and warn the cleaner about delicate buttons such as mother-of-pearl. _Frequent cleaning wears out the cloth._
- Remove labels, pins and tacking stitches from new clothes and smooth out packaging creases before wearing them. _Leftover tags make you look inattentive._ (Work / meeting)
- Protect suede from spills and send it to a specialist dry-cleaner when dirty. _Stains are hard to remove from suede at home._
- Choose very pale suede, such as cream, only if you'll look after it. _Light suede marks easily and is hard to keep clean._
- Check the drum is empty before each wash. _One forgotten item can stain a whole load._
- Wash at cool temperatures to prevent shrinking, using an oxygen-based detergent to deal with odours. _Cool washes protect fibres but may not remove smells on their own._
- Wash wool, cashmere, silk and wool-rich socks on a wool or hand-wash cycle, inside a mesh bag. _A gentle cycle and bag reduce stretching and felting._
- Dry knitwear flat on a towel over a rack, supporting the sleeves and gently reshaping it while damp. _Hanging wet wool stretches it out of shape._
- Iron shirts while still damp from the wash; mist and steam any that have dried completely. _Creases come out far more easily from damp cotton._
- Iron a shirt in order: collar, cuffs, yoke, back, front, then sleeves. _Working from small parts to large avoids re-creasing finished areas._
- Don't bother ironing jeans. _Denim is meant to look relaxed._
- Store shirts on hangers rather than folded, and hang them straight after ironing. _Folding adds creases, and hanging lets them finish drying._
- Hang trousers on felted hangers, folded along their front crease rather than the seams. _It keeps the crease sharp and stops slipping._
- Roll a tie loosely for a day or two after wearing, then store it. _Resting lets the creases from the knot drop out._
- Twice a year, clean the outgoing season's clothes, weed out what you didn't wear, and store the rest in moth-proof bags somewhere dry. _A seasonal changeover protects clothes and keeps the wardrobe manageable; humidity damages fabric._
- Brush suits after each wear and dry clean only for stains or odour. _Frequent dry cleaning slowly damages the cloth._ (Work / meeting)
- Treat loafers you wear sockless with an antifungal spray now and then. _Sweat soaking into leather encourages fungus and odour._ (Everyday)
- A hairstyle should match the mood of your clothes; work with your natural hair rather than against it. _Hair is part of the overall look._ (opinion)
